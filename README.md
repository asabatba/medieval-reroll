# Medieval Reroll

A deterministic history engine: resolve a whole medieval village's genealogy from a single address, then browse any life in it. No simulation loop — every fact is computed directly from `(world seed, region, village index, person id)`, the same address always resolving to the same life, the same way a page of the Library of Babel always holds the same text.

Live behind a CapRover deploy (`Dockerfile` / `nginx.conf`); no public URL is checked into this repo.

## Running it

```
pnpm install
pnpm dev        # local dev server
pnpm build      # typecheck + production build to dist/
pnpm test       # vitest
pnpm typecheck  # tsc --noEmit
pnpm lint       # biome
pnpm check      # biome, writing fixes
```

## Architecture

See the header comment in [src/engine/index.ts](src/engine/index.ts) for the full picture. In short:

- **Tier 1 — `resolveVillage()`** ([src/engine/village.ts](src/engine/village.ts)): one pure constraint solve per village address. Fixes the entire genealogy — households, births, deaths (with causes), marriage matching, migration. All *relational* facts (who's whose spouse/sibling/parent) live here, so they're symmetric by construction. Cached, but the cache is an optimization, not a correctness requirement: re-solving an address always reproduces the identical envelope.
- **Tier 2 — `decodePerson()`** ([src/engine/biography.ts](src/engine/biography.ts)): O(1) per-person decode. Reads relational facts from the envelope and decorates them with narrative — occupation, texture events, world events — decoded from the person's own address hash. Never invents a relational fact; locale only changes which pre-written text is picked, never which template/roll.
- **`src/engine/data/`** holds the historical grounding: regions, demography (life tables, marriage ages, mobility rates), plagues, jurisdictions, place names, narrative templates, social classes, royal lines.
- **The preventive check (§ carrying capacity)** ([src/engine/capacity.ts](src/engine/capacity.ts)): a village is bounded by its land, not by whatever its parameters happen to compound to. `holdingsOf` fixes the stock of tenements from the address; `holdingsAt` takes out what the post-plague retreat from the margin left uncultivated. The Tier-1 solve measures occupied holdings against that as it goes, and the answer moves the age of marriage, the share who never marry, and the rate of departure — with a hard edge (`HOLDINGS_FULL`) below which no new household can be founded at all, so a man waits for dead men's shoes. `EXOGAMY_SHARE` caps how many marriages may take a spouse from outside the parish, which keeps the inbound and outbound flows in the same order of magnitude. Without this the population was a free exponential: villages of eight souls by 1490 in England beside Catalan ones still growing.
- **The harvest (§ the harvest)** ([src/engine/harvest.ts](src/engine/harvest.ts)): the year that actually varied. A region used to have exactly one famine — a fixed window in `regions.ts` — and two centuries of identical weather either side of it. Now the documented failures are data ([src/engine/data/harvest.ts](src/engine/data/harvest.ts): the Great Famine, *lo mal any primer*, the Tuscan dearth of 1328–30, the English dear years of 1437–40) and the ordinary variance around them is hashed per **world**, so re-rolling the seed gives the 1350s different weather while the Great Famine stays where history put it. A failed harvest does three things: it kills (graded by depth, by age, and by whether the household had anything put by — and a second failure running is far worse, because the seed corn is gone), it **postpones weddings** by a year, and it thins the next year's baptisms. Two corrections fell out of it: the Great Famine was a north-European rain event that never reached Iberia, so Castile and Portugal now get their own crises instead of one they were never in; and the sweating sickness of 1485 is England-only rather than a world-wide wave.
- **The tenement (§ the tenement)** ([src/engine/tenement.ts](src/engine/tenement.ts)): `capacity.ts` made the land the binding constraint of the whole model and then represented it as the integer 17. Holdings now have identity and a size — virgate, half-virgate, cottage, toft, in the proportions the manorial extents show — and every household is assigned one at the moment it forms, preferring the ground the husband's father held if he is the heir and it has fallen vacant. So succession becomes a fact about a **place**, "peasant" stops covering both thirty acres and two, and a household with no holding at all is an *undersettle*, a real category the count could not express. The payoff is the one page in the app whose subject is a piece of ground: one holding and every family across it, vacancies included.
- **The season (§ the season)** ([src/engine/season.ts](src/engine/season.ts)): dates to the day, on the Julian calendar the era actually kept. The point is not the day but the *shape of the year*: canon law shut roughly a third of it to weddings, in the three Sarum closed seasons (Advent to the octave of Epiphany; Septuagesima to the octave of Easter; Rogationtide to the octave of Pentecost) — and **two of the three move with Easter**, so the file computes the Julian Easter rather than hard-coding a Lent. Mask those days out and the famous register signature falls out on its own: March and December empty of marriages, a rush in late January, and the great October–November peak after harvest. Burials answer to something else — bubonic plague is a high-summer disease, famine kills in the hungry gap before harvest, drownings peak at harvest — so the season a person died in follows from *what* killed them. Dates ride on `BioEvent.date` and `Bio.birthDate`/`deathDate` as **structured data, never written into prose**, so the engine keeps emitting plain text, the UI does the formatting, and adding all of this changed no existing sentence. `feastOf` ([src/engine/data/feasts.ts](src/engine/data/feasts.ts)) names the day the way people actually named it — Michaelmas, Martinmas, Lady Day. Only the three entries a parish register really dated (baptism, wedding, burial) carry a day; world news and court business keep the bare year, and the column is uneven on purpose.
- **The Schism (§ the Schism)** ([src/engine/papacy.ts](src/engine/papacy.ts)): the other head of the medieval world, on the same footing as the kings — real men, known dates, no dice. What makes it unlike every other lookup in the engine is that the *data is shared and the answer is not*: `popeAt("england", 1400)` and `popeAt("catalonia", 1400)` read the same three lines in [src/engine/data/papacy.ts](src/engine/data/papacy.ts) (the undivided/Roman line, the Avignon claimants, the Pisan line of 1409) through an `OBEDIENCE` table, and return different men. Between 1378 and 1417 there was no single Church, and `papalSeriesOf(region)` flattens each region's own obedience into one contiguous list — *the popes of England*, *the popes of Castile* — including the stretches where a realm recognised nobody at all (France's subtraction of obedience, 1398–1402; Aragon's neutrality until 1387). That series is a route of its own (`#seed:region:papacy`, `#seed:region:papacy:term`), every pope named in prose links into it, and jubilee years fall out of the same table.
- **The church's own line (§ the church's own line)** ([src/engine/clergy.ts](src/engine/clergy.ts)): the same construction as `manorLineOf`, applied to the institution the villagers actually stood inside every week. `rectorAt(...)` names the priest serving in any year; the line is keyed to the **parish seat**, so a shared mother church has one incumbent and not one per village. Two things in it carry real weight: whether the living is *appropriated* (a third of them are — the tithes belong to a priory, which presents a salaried **vicar** rather than a **rector**, and that decides who presented him), and the mortality of 1349, run off the plague chronology, which puts ~37% of the men serving in 1348 dead in the Great Mortality and gets some parishes through two or three incumbents inside twelve months — which is exactly why the bishops' institution registers are the best serial evidence for the Black Death that survives. Each incumbent has a page (`#seed:region:village:rector:idx`) and the register of them sits on the parish page.
- **Named epidemics (§ named epidemics)** ([src/engine/data/epidemics.ts](src/engine/data/epidemics.ts)): the same move `types.ts` records for `accident`/`violence`, applied to what was left in `disease`. Dated outbreaks (the sweating sickness of 1485, England only; the dearth fever of 1438–40; the catarrhal epidemic of 1427) and an endemic background that is a fact about *where you live* (the tertian ague of the Italian lowlands, St Anthony's fire in the French and German rye country, the flux and the consumption everywhere). This names deaths; it does not change them — wiring epidemics into the hazard would re-roll every envelope, so it waits for the harvest series.
- **Nobility (§ nobility)** ([src/engine/nobility.ts](src/engine/nobility.ts)): kings are *data*, not dice — `sovereignAt(region, year)` looks up the real sovereign lines in [src/engine/data/nobility.ts](src/engine/data/nobility.ts) (Plantagenet→Tudor, Capet→Valois, the count-kings of Aragon with the 1410–12 Interregnum, the Emperors, Florence's republic and the Medici). Noble houses are *generated*: each honour has a baronial house and each manor a lord line (`lordOfManorAt`), pure functions of the address like `parishOf`/`manorOf`, with contiguous successions across the whole register era. The manor line is anchored so its 1360 head is exactly the static `fief.lord` name; biographies cite the lord holding *in the event's year* (wardship, merchet), narrate changes of lord, and pick up royal accession news derived from the reign data.
- **`src/ui/`** is a small hash-routed vanilla-TS front end; `src/i18n/` holds the (English/Catalan) UI strings and locale persistence. Besides person records (`#seed:region:village:person`), the nobility and the Church have fixed URLs of their own: `#seed:region:royal` (the royal line), `#seed:region:royal:reign` (one sovereign's page), `#seed:region:papacy` (the popes this region obeyed), `#seed:region:papacy:term` (one pontificate — or one vacancy, or one stretch of obeying nobody), `#seed:region:village:house` (a manor's noble house), `#seed:region:village:lord|baron:head` (one lord's page), and `#seed:region:village:rector:head` (one parish incumbent's), and `#seed:region:village:tenement:idx` (one holding, and every family that ever stood on it). Two figures carry the two things hardest to say in prose: the **population curve** on a village's page, and the **lifeline** on a person's — one life drawn against the plague waves, famines and wars that crossed it, with a tick for every dated entry. Both are single-series by design: this palette's gilt and rubric are 0.3 ΔE apart under deuteranopia in the light theme, so no figure asks a reader to tell them apart inside one plot, and the famine band carries a hatch as well as a hue. Everything the UI names should be a link: kings and lords mentioned in chronicle prose link to their *own pages* through the same `EventRef` mechanism that links people (`route` + `routeIdx`), with aliases like "King Henry" resolved year-aware to the sovereign actually reigning at the event's date.

## Adding a region

Regions are the main axis of expansion. To add one:

1. Add an entry to `REGIONS` in [src/engine/data/regions.ts](src/engine/data/regions.ts) — names, surnames, place-name components, marriage-age windows, famine/war years, inheritance custom (`impartible`/`partible`).
2. Add a matching entry to `DEMOGRAPHY` in [src/engine/data/demography.ts](src/engine/data/demography.ts) (or rely on `DEFAULT_DEMOGRAPHY`).
3. Add jurisdiction/fief naming data in [src/engine/data/jurisdictions.ts](src/engine/data/jurisdictions.ts).
4. Add the region's real royal line to `ROYAL_LINES` in [src/engine/data/nobility.ts](src/engine/data/nobility.ts) — reigns must cover 1235–1500 with no gaps (interregna are explicit entries); notable transitions can carry a hand-written `accession` text.
5. If the region took a side in the Great Schism, add an entry to `OBEDIENCE` in [src/engine/data/papacy.ts](src/engine/data/papacy.ts). Nothing is required: a region with no entry simply held to the undivided line throughout, which is what `papalSeriesOf` assumes, and it still gets the elections and the Schism itself as news. Spans only ever cover 1378–1418, and `null` means the realm recognised no pope at all.
6. Nothing is needed for the parish clergy or the epidemics: [src/engine/clergy.ts](src/engine/clergy.ts) generates a line from the region's own name pools, and [src/engine/data/epidemics.ts](src/engine/data/epidemics.ts)'s ungated entries apply everywhere. Add a `regions`-filtered epidemic only where the new region has a local sickness the record actually names.
7. A handful of narrative templates in [src/engine/data/narrative.ts](src/engine/data/narrative.ts) are region-gated (`englandM`, region-name checks); add region-specific texture there if it's warranted, but most templates are shared across all regions by default.

`regionRank`/`REGION_ORDER` ([src/engine/rank.ts](src/engine/rank.ts)) is derived from `Object.keys(REGIONS)`, so a new region automatically slots in as the highest-ranked (only relevant to which regions can pull long-distance migrants from which).

## Testing conventions

Tests scan real `resolveVillage()` output for the scenario under test rather than hand-building `Envelope`/`Person` fixtures — see `succession.test.ts` or `lineage.test.ts` for the pattern. This keeps tests honest about what the engine can actually produce.
